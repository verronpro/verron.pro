  My first son was born on Monday evening. The day, the first week, and what I now understand my role to be.

At 10 AM, my wife asked me whether her water might have broken. There was no gushing, no contractions, nothing else yet.

At 10:40 we were at the hospital for a check. At 11 we were in maternity. At 21:51 our son was born, healthy. His mother was still surprised by him.

The moment I remember most is when they took her into the delivery room. I was not allowed to accompany her. A public hospital in China is very efficient. It is also very different from home.

My wife had pushed to have me with her instead of her mother while she waited for delivery. The nurses wanted a Chinese-speaking family to support her because that would have made their work easier. She wanted me there. I am grateful for that.

The first night was long. The maternity ward did not let me sleep beside my wife. I could not fall asleep, so I worked on my personal project to tire myself out.

My wife and I decided on our son's French name months ago. Her Chinese family is much more involved in his Chinese name. On Saturday it was still being debated, and the delay is now putting the administrative steps with the French embassy at risk.

My family in France was happy. The distance made that happiness hard to share. At work, I sent the inner circle of colleagues one picture chosen for publication, with the weight and size: 3 kg 930, 52 cm. They were curious about what a mixed baby looks like.

I had been afraid of a very complicated aftermath and was ready for long, intense weeks. It has been easier than I feared.

The maternity ward had a recommended ayi/doula service. It helped us during the first two days and nights and taught us the first gestures. Separately, my wife and her parents had already booked a doula for one month, for when we came back home on Wednesday. This kind of doula service is common practice in China.

My parents-in-law did a lot. My mother-in-law sometimes wanted me away so she could do things her way. I had to fight to stay relevant.

I now see my role more clearly. I do not need to be there 24/7. My job is to give emotional support, help my son get used to French, and learn and practise the gestures in case I have to do them myself. Providing for the years to come is part of it too.

At home, the first useful things were the bottle, the milk powder, and hip-drying cream. The stroller and baby carrier will definitely be useful in time. The pump has been tried, but has not been adopted yet. I do not think we bought anything unnecessary.

This post is dated Monday because that is when my son arrived. I am writing it on Sunday morning. Yesterday he returned his food on my shoulder for the first time. I made lasagna for the whole family. That was my cultural contribution for the day. This morning I made pain perdu.
