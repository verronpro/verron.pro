Two things got settled in September. The product got a name, Ember字, and a
licence that reflects what it actually is.

The name matters more than it looks. For months the project went by a working
title, and a working title leaks into everything: the app id, the scheme, the
launcher label, the docs. Renaming it means touching all of those, and getting
them to agree.

The licence matters more. The scaffolding tools had copied in a boilerplate
open-source notice that no one here ever wrote or endorsed. It said things about
the code, the corpus, and the decks that weren't true. Replacing it with a
proprietary notice was mostly about writing down what was already the case: this
is first-party work, the corpus is ours, and the third-party material keeps its
own licences.
